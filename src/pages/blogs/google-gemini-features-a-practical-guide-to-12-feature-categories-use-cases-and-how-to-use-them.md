## Introduction

Trying every Google Gemini feature takes weeks. Most people burn an hour on the wrong one, decide Gemini does not work, and never come back. The fix is picking the single feature that matches the task you already do every day.

Google Gemini is Google's AI assistant, available on the web, on mobile, and on desktop. Behind one interface it bundles a family of models, image and video generation, deep research, coding tools, voice conversations, and connectors to apps like Gmail and Drive. The problem is that these features have different names, different availability rules, and different subscription requirements, so knowing what to open and when is most of the battle.

This guide covers 12 useful Gemini feature categories. For each one, you get what it does, when it is useful, and the exact steps to start.

**Official reference:** [Google Gemini Apps Release Notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/)

> **Note:** Feature availability, usage limits, supported languages, and subscription requirements may vary by region, account, device, and rollout status.

## How to start: pick one workflow and test it in 10 minutes

The fastest way to learn Gemini is to run one real task end to end, not to read about all 12 features.

1. Choose one goal, such as writing, research, coding, or learning.
2. Open Gemini and explain the result you want.
3. Check the answer before you use or share it.

Do not try all 12 features at once. Learn one useful workflow first, then add another.

### Choose a starting point

- **Need an explanation?** Try advanced reasoning or Education and Learning.
- **Need a visual?** Try Image, Video, and Music Creation.
- **Need current information?** Try Research and Information Analysis.
- **Need to build something?** Try Coding and Prototyping.
- **Need to organize work?** Try Productivity or connected apps.

## 1. AI Models and Advanced Reasoning for complex, multi-step analysis

**Best for:** Difficult questions, calculations, comparisons, and planning.

Google Gemini offers different models for different kinds of work. Some are best for quick answers, while others are better for difficult tasks that need more careful thinking.

You can use these models to solve problems, work with numbers, review code, understand images, and combine information from several sources.

### Use Cases

- Solve complex mathematical and logical problems.
- Analyze business reports and financial documents.
- Compare different strategies and evaluate alternatives.
- Understand scientific and technical concepts.
- Plan complicated projects with multiple dependencies.
- Analyze several documents and summarize their combined findings.

**Example prompt:**

> Analyze these three business reports. Identify the major trends, compare their financial performance, highlight potential risks, and recommend five actions for the next quarter. Explain your reasoning and cite the relevant figures from the documents.

### When to Use

Use advanced reasoning when a task needs several steps, careful comparison, calculations, or deeper analysis.

Choose a faster model for simple questions, summaries, brainstorming, and everyday writing. Choose a stronger reasoning model when the task is complex or needs more detail.

### How to Use

1. Open [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/).
2. Start a new conversation.
3. Select the appropriate model or reasoning mode if a model selector is available.
4. Explain your objective, relevant context, and constraints.
5. Upload supporting documents or images when needed.
6. Ask Gemini to explain its conclusions, check calculations, and identify uncertainties.

**Tip:** Break complicated tasks into smaller stages. Ask Gemini to analyze the evidence first, evaluate possible solutions second, and produce the final recommendation last.

## 2. Image, Video, and Music Creation for fast visual drafts

**Best for:** Quick visual ideas, social posts, mockups, and creative drafts.

Gemini can help you create and edit visual and audio content. Depending on what is available to you, it can make images, illustrations, video ideas, and other creative assets from your instructions.

Some creative features may need a Google AI subscription or a connected Google service.

### Use Cases

- Create illustrations for blog posts and websites.
- Generate social media graphics and promotional concepts.
- Design product mockups and advertising visuals.
- Edit photos using natural-language instructions.
- Combine multiple images into a new composition.
- Generate video concepts, clips, or personalized visual content where supported.
- Explore soundtrack and music-generation capabilities available through supported tools.

**Example prompt for image creation:**

> Create a clean, modern hero image for a technology blog about artificial intelligence. Use a futuristic digital workspace, subtle gradients, realistic lighting, and a professional editorial style. Leave negative space on the left for a headline.

**Example prompt for video creation:**

> Create a short promotional video for a sustainable skincare brand. Show natural ingredients, elegant product packaging, soft daylight, and a clean visual style. Keep the scenes consistent and end with a simple product shot.

### When to Use

Use these features when you need visual assets, creative inspiration, advertising concepts, or media prototypes without beginning every project from scratch.

They are especially useful for marketers, designers, content creators, educators, and small businesses that need to produce multiple creative variations quickly.

### How to Use

1. Open Gemini and locate the image or video creation option available to your account.
2. Describe the subject, style, intended audience, and desired output.
3. Specify the composition, lighting, colors, format, and any restrictions.
4. Generate the initial result.
5. Request focused changes rather than rewriting the entire prompt.
6. Download or export the result when the interface supports it.
7. Review the output for visual errors, factual issues, branding, and usage rights.

**Tip:** Treat AI-generated media as a first draft. Review logos, text, faces, product details, and other elements that must be accurate.

## 3. Research, Search, and Information Analysis for multi-source reports

**Best for:** Learning about a topic, comparing sources, and making reports.

Gemini can help with online research, document analysis, and clear explanations. Deep Research is useful when you need to study a topic using several sources and turn the findings into a report.

Depending on the task and available tools, Gemini can also help analyze uploaded files, interpret supported media, and produce summaries, comparisons, or visual explanations.

### Use Cases

- Research competitors and industry trends.
- Prepare market research reports.
- Summarize academic papers and technical documents.
- Compare products, services, and business strategies.
- Investigate a topic using multiple online sources.
- Extract key findings from long documents.
- Prepare research briefs with questions for further investigation.

**Example prompt:**

> Research the current state of AI-powered customer support software. Compare five major solutions by capabilities, integrations, pricing models, limitations, and target customers. Prioritize official product documentation and recent sources. Include source links and identify any information that could not be verified.

### When to Use

Use research features when you need several sources, current information, a detailed comparison, or a proper report.

Deep Research is appropriate for broad questions that benefit from investigation and synthesis. A normal chat is usually sufficient for simple definitions or questions that do not require extensive research.

### How to Use

1. Open Gemini.
2. Select Deep Research if it is available and appropriate for your task.
3. Define the research question and scope.
4. Specify the audience, timeframe, geographic focus, and preferred source types.
5. Review the proposed research approach if Gemini provides one.
6. Let Gemini gather and synthesize information.
7. Check the source links, dates, and evidence supporting important claims.
8. Refine the report or export it using the available options.

**Tip:** Ask Gemini to distinguish verified facts from estimates, interpretations, and unresolved questions. Always check primary sources for important decisions.

## 4. Coding, Prototyping, and Application Development for writing and debugging code

**Best for:** Learning code, fixing errors, and building small prototypes.

Gemini can help both developers and beginners write code, understand programming ideas, fix errors, and build prototypes. Canvas gives you a place to work on supported code and content. Some eligible macOS users may also have access to Google Antigravity for agent-based coding.

These tools can help you turn an idea into a working prototype faster.

### Use Cases

- Build simple web applications.
- Generate HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Python code.
- Debug programming errors.
- Explain unfamiliar codebases.
- Create interactive calculators and dashboards.
- Prototype landing pages and internal tools.
- Generate test cases and documentation.
- Explore application ideas before investing in full development.

**Example prompt:**

> Build a responsive task-management web application using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Include task creation, editing, deletion, completion status, and filtering. Use accessible controls and a clean interface. Explain the file structure and provide instructions for running the application locally.

### When to Use

Use Gemini for coding when you need to understand an unfamiliar programming concept, accelerate repetitive development work, prototype an idea, or investigate an error.

It is particularly useful during early development, when requirements are changing and you need to explore several possible implementations.

### How to Use

1. Describe the application or programming problem.
2. Specify the language, framework, environment, and constraints.
3. Ask Gemini to propose an implementation plan.
4. Generate the code in the chat, Canvas, or another supported coding environment.
5. Run the code in an appropriate development environment.
6. Share errors, logs, or unexpected behavior with Gemini.
7. Ask for targeted fixes and tests.
8. Review the code for security, correctness, performance, and maintainability before deployment.

**Tip:** Never assume generated code is production-ready. Test it, inspect dependencies, protect secrets, and verify authentication, authorization, and input validation.

## 5. AI Agents and Task Automation for multi-step, repeated work

**Best for:** Workflows with several steps or repeated actions.

Agent-based Gemini features can help you complete several connected steps instead of only giving you instructions. Depending on what is available, an agent may help with research, connected apps, local files, documents, or coding tasks.

Gemini Spark and Gemini Agent are examples of agent-oriented experiences documented in Google's release history. They have different eligibility and availability requirements.

### Use Cases

- Organize files and create project documents.
- Research a topic and compile a report.
- Coordinate information across supported Google applications.
- Automate repetitive administrative tasks.
- Prepare project plans and supporting materials.
- Build and test software through supported coding agents.
- Create repeatable workflows for recurring business processes.

**Example prompt:**

> Help me prepare a weekly project review. Review the project documents I provide, summarize completed work, identify overdue items, list unresolved risks, and draft an update for stakeholders. Show me the proposed actions before making changes or sending anything.

### When to Use

Use agent-based workflows when a task involves several connected steps, multiple information sources, or repeated actions that would otherwise require manual coordination.

For sensitive activities, such as sending emails, changing files, or purchasing products, maintain human oversight and require confirmation where appropriate.

### How to Use

1. Check which agent or automation features are available in your Gemini account.
2. Define the desired outcome clearly.
3. Provide the necessary context, files, and authorized app connections.
4. Specify the tasks the agent may perform and any actions that require approval.
5. Review the proposed plan when the interface provides one.
6. Monitor progress and intervene if the agent takes an incorrect direction.
7. Verify the completed work before using or sharing it.

**Tip:** Begin with a low-risk workflow. Once you understand its reliability, gradually introduce more complex tasks and explicit approval checkpoints.

## 6. Voice, Conversation, and Multimodal Interaction for talking through ideas

**Best for:** Talking through ideas, practising languages, and understanding images.

Gemini Live supports natural, back-and-forth voice conversations on supported devices. Gemini can also interpret supported images, documents, audio, and video, depending on the feature being used.

These features are useful when typing is inconvenient or when it is easier to show Gemini something than explain it with words.

### Use Cases

- Practice speaking a foreign language.
- Brainstorm ideas through voice conversation.
- Ask questions about an image or diagram.
- Get help understanding a chart or screenshot.
- Discuss a document while reviewing its contents.
- Transcribe or summarize supported audio content.
- Receive explanations of unfamiliar concepts through conversation.

**Example prompt:**

> Act as my conversational English tutor. Ask me one question at a time about everyday situations. After each response, identify my major grammar mistakes, suggest a more natural version, and continue the conversation at an intermediate level.

### When to Use

Use voice and multimodal features when you are away from a keyboard, need a conversational learning experience, or want Gemini to interpret visual information.

They are also useful when describing an object or situation in words would be more difficult than showing it.

### How to Use

1. Open the Gemini app on a supported device.
2. Start Gemini Live or another available voice experience.
3. Speak naturally and explain what you want to accomplish.
4. Share an image, document, or supported visual context when appropriate.
5. Ask follow-up questions to clarify the response.
6. Switch between voice and text if your version supports it.

**Tip:** Avoid sharing confidential information, private conversations, or sensitive images unless you understand the relevant privacy settings and have permission to share them.

## 7. Personalization and Memory so you stop repeating yourself

**Best for:** Getting answers in your preferred style without repeating instructions.

Gemini offers personalization features that can help it tailor responses to user preferences and context. Depending on account eligibility and settings, these can include saved information, previous conversations, and Personal Intelligence through supported Google app connections.

Personalization can save you from repeating the same instructions. Still, review your connected apps and check what information Gemini can use.

### Use Cases

- Maintain a consistent writing style.
- Remember project preferences and recurring requirements.
- Tailor explanations to a preferred skill level.
- Use relevant context from supported Google applications.
- Continue ongoing work without repeating background information.
- Search previous conversations for useful prompts or decisions.

**Example prompt:**

> For my future marketing drafts, use a clear and professional tone, short paragraphs, practical examples, and straightforward language. Avoid exaggerated claims. When you make recommendations, explain the reasoning and distinguish assumptions from confirmed information.

### When to Use

Use personalization when you frequently work on similar projects or want Gemini to adapt its responses to your preferred style and workflow.

Avoid relying on saved context for information that must be current, exact, or independently verified. Confirm important details before using them in a decision.

### How to Use

1. Open Gemini settings.
2. Review the available personalization, saved information, or memory controls.
3. Enable only the features you want to use.
4. Connect supported Google applications if you want Gemini to use their information.
5. Provide clear preferences and corrections.
6. Review, update, or disable personalization settings as needed.

**Tip:** Keep persistent preferences general and useful. Do not store passwords, authentication codes, or other sensitive information in prompts or saved instructions.

## 8. Google Apps and Third-Party Integrations for context from Gmail, Drive, and more

**Best for:** Finding and organizing information across connected apps.

Gemini can connect to supported Google apps and some third-party services. This lets it use information from those tools and help you complete related tasks.

Available integrations and actions vary by region, account type, language, and service.

### Use Cases

- Find relevant information in Gmail.
- Summarize documents stored in Google Drive.
- Work with information from supported Google applications.
- Review schedules and organize activities.
- Find places or explore location-related information.
- Create visual content through supported creative integrations.
- Assist with grocery orders or restaurant reservations where supported.
- Combine information from multiple connected services.

**Example prompt:**

> Using the Google applications I have connected and authorized, help me prepare for tomorrow's meetings. Find the relevant calendar events, identify related emails and documents, summarize the key discussion points, and create a concise preparation checklist. Ask before making any changes or sending messages.

### When to Use

Use connected apps when the information you need already exists in another service or when your task requires coordinating information across multiple tools.

They are especially helpful for workflows involving email, documents, calendars, research, and supported external services.

### How to Use

1. Open Gemini's settings or Customize section.
2. Find the available connected apps.
3. Review the permissions and connect the services you want to use.
4. Ask Gemini to perform a specific task using the relevant information.
5. Clarify which documents, time periods, or records it should consider.
6. Review the results and verify important details.
7. Confirm any consequential action before it is completed.

**Tip:** Grant only the access necessary for the task. An integration's availability does not necessarily mean every action within the connected application is supported.

## 9. Productivity and Everyday Assistance for drafts, summaries, and planning

**Best for:** Turning rough notes into clear work.

Gemini can help with everyday work such as writing, summarizing, planning, organizing information, and preparing messages. Features such as Daily Brief and desktop assistance can help you work without switching between apps all the time.

### Use Cases

- Draft emails, reports, proposals, and meeting notes.
- Summarize lengthy documents.
- Extract action items from notes.
- Create daily or weekly work plans.
- Turn rough ideas into structured outlines.
- Prepare agendas and project updates.
- Rewrite text for a particular audience.
- Organize tasks by priority and deadline.

**Example prompt:**

> Turn these meeting notes into a professional summary. Organize the output into decisions made, action items, owners, deadlines, unresolved questions, and risks. Mark any missing owners or deadlines as unspecified instead of inventing them.

### When to Use

Use Gemini when you have information that needs to be organized, rewritten, summarized, or transformed into a practical deliverable.

It is particularly useful for repetitive writing and administrative work, as well as preparing a first draft that you can review and finalize.

### How to Use

1. Identify the deliverable you need.
2. Provide the source text, notes, files, or relevant context.
3. Specify the intended audience and preferred format.
4. Ask for the desired structure, length, and tone.
5. Review the draft for completeness and accuracy.
6. Request targeted revisions.
7. Copy, export, or transfer the final output using the available options.

**Tip:** Tell Gemini what a successful output should contain. Specific requirements usually produce more useful results than a vague request to make something better.

## 10. Education and Learning for explanations, quizzes, and study plans

**Best for:** Explanations, practice questions, revision, and study plans.

Gemini can act as a study partner. It can explain difficult ideas, create practice questions, summarize notes, and help you review a subject.

Features documented in Google's release notes include Guided Learning experiences, study notebooks, a student hub, and tools for generating study materials where available.

### Use Cases

- Explain difficult topics at different levels.
- Create quizzes and practice exams.
- Generate flashcards from notes.
- Summarize textbooks and lecture materials.
- Build revision plans for examinations.
- Explain diagrams and scientific processes.
- Practice languages and conversational skills.
- Organize course resources and learning objectives.

**Example prompt:**

> Help me study introductory statistics. First, assess my understanding of mean, median, variance, and standard deviation. Then explain each concept with a simple example, give me five practice questions, and provide feedback on my answers without immediately revealing the solutions.

### When to Use

Use Gemini when you need personalized explanations, additional practice, structured revision, or help turning a large volume of learning material into manageable study sessions.

It is useful for independent learners, students, teachers, and professionals learning new skills.

### How to Use

1. Identify the subject and your current knowledge level.
2. Upload relevant notes or course materials if appropriate.
3. Ask Gemini to create a structured learning plan.
4. Request explanations, examples, quizzes, or flashcards.
5. Answer practice questions before reviewing the solutions.
6. Ask Gemini to explain mistakes and recommend further practice.
7. Verify important academic facts against reliable course materials.

**Tip:** Use Gemini as an interactive tutor rather than just an answer generator. Explaining your reasoning and solving problems yourself will help you learn more effectively.

## 11. Gems, Skills, and Custom AI Assistants for tasks you repeat with the same rules

**Best for:** Repeating the same task with the same rules and format.

Gemini customization tools let you save instructions for tasks you do often. Gems can act as specialized assistants, while Skills can reuse a saved workflow when your prompt matches it. The exact names and options may change over time as Google transitions between these features.

### Use Cases

- Create a consistent brand-writing workflow.
- Build an assistant for blog outlining and editing.
- Standardize product-description generation.
- Apply formatting rules to recurring reports.
- Create a study assistant for a specific subject.
- Reuse a standard checklist for project reviews.
- Combine writing instructions with brand guidelines.

**Example prompt for a reusable skill:**

> When I ask you to draft a blog post, first identify the target audience and search intent. Then create an SEO-friendly outline, write a practical introduction, organize the content into clear sections, include examples and actionable steps, and finish with a concise conclusion. Avoid keyword stuffing and unsupported claims.

### When to Use

Use custom assistants or skills when you repeat the same task frequently and want consistent formatting, tone, structure, or decision rules.

They are especially useful for content teams, marketing workflows, customer support, reporting, and other repeatable processes.

### How to Use

1. Open Gemini's Customize section or the relevant Gem or Skills interface.
2. Explore available templates or create a custom configuration.
3. Describe the assistant's purpose and expected behavior.
4. Include instructions, examples, formatting rules, and restrictions.
5. Test the configuration using realistic prompts.
6. Refine the instructions when the results are inconsistent.
7. Save or share the configuration if supported.

**Tip:** Write instructions as a reusable procedure. Explain what the assistant should do, what it should avoid, and how the final output should be formatted.

## 12. Desktop, Browser, and Mobile Experiences for help without leaving your workflow

**Best for:** Getting quick help without leaving your current device or app.

Gemini is available through web and mobile experiences, with dedicated desktop applications and browser integrations also documented in the release notes. The available features differ by operating system, account, and region.

These options let you use Gemini while working in the apps and devices you already use.

### Use Cases

- Ask questions without leaving your current workflow.
- Draft messages while working in other applications.
- Use voice dictation to produce polished text.
- Get help understanding supported screen content.
- Summarize information from connected Google apps.
- Use Gemini in Chrome to assist with web research.
- Explore coding workflows through supported desktop integrations.
- Access voice assistance while using compatible mobile experiences.

**Example prompt:**

> Review the text I have selected and rewrite it to be clearer and more professional. Preserve its meaning, keep the length similar, and return only the revised version.

### When to Use

Use desktop or browser integrations when you want AI assistance close to the application where you are working.

Use mobile features when you need conversational assistance, quick research, or help while away from your computer.

### How to Use

1. Visit the official Gemini website or the relevant app download page.
2. Check the operating system and account requirements.
3. Install the appropriate application or open Gemini in your browser.
4. Sign in and review the available permissions.
5. Configure supported keyboard shortcuts, voice controls, or app connections.
6. Provide the relevant prompt or share supported screen context.
7. Review the response before transferring it into your work.

**Tip:** Screen context and desktop integrations may expose information visible in the current application. Share only the context needed for your task and review privacy settings before enabling these features.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Gemini free to use?

Yes. A free account covers chat, image understanding, and many of the features in this guide, with usage limits. Features like Deep Research, advanced image and video generation, and higher usage caps may require a Google AI subscription. Check the feature's availability notice inside Gemini for your account.

### What is the difference between Google Gemini and ChatGPT?

Both are AI assistants with chat, image generation, coding, and research tools. Gemini's distinct advantages are deep integration with Google apps like Gmail, Drive, and Calendar, and a free tier that is generous for everyday tasks. ChatGPT may suit you better if your team already lives in its ecosystem. For most individual users already using Google services, Gemini is the lower-friction choice.

### Can Gemini search the internet and use current information?

Yes. Gemini can pull in current information from the web, and the Deep Research mode is built specifically for multi-source research reports. For any claim that matters, open the source links Gemini provides and verify them, because answers can still contain outdated or incorrect details.

### Do I need a paid plan to use the 12 features in this guide?

No. Most features work on the free tier within usage limits. A paid Google AI plan mainly raises those limits and opens the heavier tools, such as advanced video generation and larger research jobs. Start free, and upgrade only when a specific feature becomes part of your weekly workflow.
